# Initial Call Script: West Michigan Manufacturing Lead Outreach

**Goal:** Book a 15-minute site audit or intro call. Do not sell hard. Qualify interest and gather info about current web presence and pain.

---

## OPENING (30 seconds)

### If reaching the OWNER:
> "Hi [Name], this is Blake. I work with precision machine shops and tool makers in the area—I shoot professional photos of the work you do and build modern websites for shops that don't have one yet or are running something really outdated. Do you have 2 minutes?"

### If reaching OFFICE ADMIN/Front-Desk:
> "Hi [Name], I'm Blake. I reach out to shops in Muskegon and Holland with professional photography and website services. Is this a good time for a quick question, or should I call back?"

**If they say yes → move to DISCOVERY. If they say "no thanks" or "not interested" → ask for a better contact time and move on.**

---

## DISCOVERY (2–3 minutes)

### ALWAYS START HERE (regardless of persona):
- "Do you have a website right now?"

### If YES (has a website):
- "How old is it? Roughly."
- "Is it mobile-friendly? Can you view it on a phone?"
- "Who handles updates to it?"

### If NO (no website):
- "How do people find you online right now?"
- "Has that been an issue for you?"

### FOLLOW-UP (everyone):
- "Have you ever thought about getting professional photos of your shop or equipment taken?"
- *Listen. If they've thought about it, ask:* "What stopped you?"

---

## THE PITCH (1 minute)

**OWNER:**
> "Here's what I do: I come out, shoot professional photos of your work and equipment—drone and ground shots—then I build you a clean, modern website that shows off what you do. It's $3,000 upfront for photos and the site build, then $200 a month for hosting, updates, and keeping it running. Most shops like yours say their site brings in calls and makes them look more professional. Want me to come by and do a quick walk-through to see if it makes sense?"

**ADMIN (reading off to owner or deciding):
> "We offer professional photography and website services. It's $3k for photos and building a site, then $200/month for hosting and maintenance. It's designed so you don't have to think about it—we keep it running. I can send over some examples of sites we've built and the owner can decide if it's interesting."

---

## HANDLE OBJECTIONS (light version)

**"We already have a website."**
> "Got it. Is it mobile-friendly? A lot of shops I talk to built theirs 5+ years ago and they don't show up on phones the same way anymore. That's a quick thing to check. I could swing by and give you an honest look—no charge—and just let you know if it's worth updating."

**"$200/month is too much."**
> "I get that. The idea is you don't have to worry about it—I handle updates, photos for new equipment, keeping it secure. A lot of shops would pay that once to someone else anyway. But I get it's not for everyone."

**"We'll do it in-house / I have a nephew who codes."**
> "That makes sense. If you ever change your mind or want professional photos for sales materials, that's the thing I'm best at. Here's my number—feel free to reach out."

**"Can you send me something to look at?"**
> "I can—but honestly, it's better if I just swing by for 15 minutes, take a quick look at what you're doing, and we can talk about whether it's a fit. No obligation. When's good for you next week?"

---

## CLOSING (ask for one of these)

### BEST CASE:
> "Cool. Let me grab a couple photos of the shop and equipment, and I'll put together a quick mockup so you can see what it'd look like. Can I swing by [Tuesday/Wednesday] afternoon?"

### SECOND BEST:
> "What if I just come by for 15 minutes, do a quick walkthrough, and tell you what I'd build? No cost. When's good next week?"

### THIRD BEST (not interested but warm):
> "Got it. Do you mind if I follow up in a couple months? Sometimes these things land at the right time. Or if you know another shop in the area doing serious work that might be interested, I'm all ears."

### KILL (not interested, cold):
> "No problem. I appreciate your time. Take care."

---

## NOTES FOR PHONE VS. EMAIL

**Phone calls:**
- Open with the photography hook first (it's tangible and warm).
- Keep it conversational, not robotic.
- If they're not the decision-maker, ask: "Is [Owner] around?" or "Who usually handles decisions on stuff like this?"

**If you get voicemail:**
> "Hi [Name], this is Blake. I'm calling because I work with precision shops in Muskegon and Holland, shooting professional photos and building modern websites. I thought it might be interesting for [Company]. Give me a call back at [number] if you want to chat. Thanks."

---

## AFTER THE CALL

**Log these for follow-up:**
- Name and title of person you spoke with
- Do they have a website? (Yes / No / Outdated)
- Stated interest level (High / Medium / Low / Not interested)
- Next step agreed (Site audit / Mockup / Call back date / Referral)
- Any pain points they mentioned (slow web presence, outdated site, manual inquiries, etc.)
- Referrals offered (ask every call: "Do you know anyone else in the area doing good work?")
